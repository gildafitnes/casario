The phases of Casa Río

Casa Río is a long-term project.

The first objective is to acquire a permanent space in Madrid, remove it from the logic of temporary rent and speculation, and make it available for cultural and collective use.

The project develops in stages. Each stage has its own budget, documentation and public record.

01 — Build Casa Río

((We are here.))

The first phase begins before Casa Río owns a building.

The objective is to create the structure, community and financial base that will eventually make acquisition possible.

1.1 — Start the fund

The first contributions create the initial Casa Río fund.

Until Casa Río has its own legal entity, contributions are received by the promoter of the project through an account used specifically for Casa Río and recorded separately from personal expenditure.

Every contribution and every project expense will form part of the public financial archive.

[How are contributions managed? →]

1.2 — Create the association

One of the first things funded by Casa Río will be Casa Río itself.

The project will establish its legal structure as an association, including registration, professional administrative support, accounting and the initial costs required to operate correctly.

Once established, Casa Río will have its own bank account and accounting system.

[Legal structure and costs →]

1.3 — Build the public archive

Casa Río will document its development from the beginning.

Budgets, expenditure, income, property research, quotations, decisions and changes to the project will remain accessible through an online archive.

Previous reports will not disappear when plans change.

The archive will become the public memory of Casa Río.

1.4 — Casa Río outside Casa Río

Casa Río does not need to wait for a building before it can exist.

Exhibitions, workshops, talks, performances, publications, collaborations and fundraising events can already happen throughout Madrid.

These events are part of what Casa Río is becoming, and something you can already be part of.

1.5 — Follow the property market

Casa Río will continuously study the spaces available in Madrid.

The current search is oriented towards approximately 45–80 m², although size, location and possibilities of use will ultimately be considered together.

Property prices, available spaces and different purchase scenarios will be periodically reviewed and archived.

The fundraising target will therefore evolve with the real market rather than remaining an arbitrary fixed number.

1.6 — Build the acquisition fund

As the project grows, an increasing proportion of its resources will accumulate in the acquisition fund.

Donations, fundraising events, collaborations, publications, art sales and other activities can all contribute.

The objective is to reach a financial position where searching for a property is no longer theoretical but realistically actionable.

02 — Find the space

Once the fund makes acquisition realistically possible, Casa Río will begin an active search for a permanent space.

Price and size are only part of the decision.

Every potential property will also be evaluated according to accessibility, street access, sound conditions, ventilation, ceiling height, neighbouring uses, renovation requirements and its ability to accommodate the different activities Casa Río wants to develop.

The objective is to find a space capable of functioning as a cultural venue, exhibition space, workshop, shop and meeting place, while also allowing — where technically and legally possible — activities such as a café, bar or food service that can help sustain the project.

Above all, Casa Río should be a place where people can meet, stay and participate without consumption being a requirement.

Properties seriously considered will be documented publicly, together with the reasons for accepting or rejecting them.

The search may take time.

Until the right space can be acquired, Casa Río will continue operating as an itinerant project: organising exhibitions, workshops, talks, performances, publications and collaborations across the city while continuing to raise funds and move towards the acquisition of a permanent home.

03 — Acquire and prepare the house

When the right property is found, Casa Río buys it.

The acquisition budget will include more than the price advertised by the seller.

It must also account for taxes, legal and technical work, notary and registration costs, renovations, accessibility, installations, licences and permissions, equipment and an initial operating reserve.

The objective is not simply to acquire four walls.

Casa Río should open with enough financial stability to begin operating without immediately being forced to monetise every activity in the building.

Before the purchase is completed, the provisional estimates used during fundraising will be replaced by real quotations, technical reports and a detailed acquisition budget.

04 — Open Casa Río

Once the space has been acquired and prepared, Casa Río opens as a permanent cultural and civic space.

Its programme can include exhibitions, artist residencies, workshops, talks, performances, publishing, printing, meetings, research, collective projects and other forms of cultural production.

The space may also incorporate the sale of books, publications, editions and artworks, alongside café, bar or food activity where permitted.

Casa Río will combine different economic models.

Casa Río will sustain itself through a mixture of open access, affordable participation and activities that generate income, from workshops and editions to commissions, hospitality and other uses of the space.

Every three months, Casa Río will review its operating costs, available reserves and expected income. This will determine how much of the programme needs to generate revenue during the following period and how much can remain free or subsidised.

Prices may vary according to the scale, capacity and cost of each activity, while artists and workers can still be paid even when participation is free for the public.

Once the financial needs of that period are covered, there is less pressure for the remaining programme to generate income.

The objective is not to maximise revenue from every person entering Casa Río, but to generate enough to sustain the house while protecting as much of its programme as possible from the obligation to make money.

Casa Río is conceived not primarily as a place of consumption, but as a place of coexistence: somewhere people can meet, stay, participate and spend time together without having to buy something in order to be there.

05 — Make Casa Río self-sufficient

Owning the building removes rent, but it does not remove the cost of maintaining a house.

Every year Casa Río will publish an operating budget covering salaries, utilities, insurance, taxes, administration, maintenance, programme production, repairs and reserves.

The following year's budget will also be estimated in advance, including expected changes in costs and inflation.

Actual figures will progressively replace estimates as the year develops.

Casa Río will also publish its monthly financial position: what the house currently costs, how much has already been generated and what still needs to be covered.

Income can come from many places: café and food, workshops, art commissions, publications, retail, events, recurring support and other activities.

When one area generates more income, more of the programme can remain outside the need to generate money directly.

Casa Río will be considered fully self-sufficient when its normal activity can cover its operating costs and the project has accumulated approximately two complete years of essential operating expenses in reserve.

That reserve protects the house from crises, repairs, unexpected costs and periods of lower income.

It also marks an important transition.

Casa Río no longer needs to concentrate exclusively on protecting itself.

It can begin thinking about another space.

06 — Begin the next Casa Río

Casa Río is not necessarily a project for a single building.

Once the first house is economically stable, its operating costs are covered and two years of essential reserves have been secured, the process can begin again.

A new fund can be created, Casa Río 2 can start.

A new property can be researched.

A new part of the city can be considered.

The experience, documentation, legal structures and economic knowledge developed through the first Casa Río become the starting infrastructure for the next one.

The long-term objective is therefore larger than protecting one cultural space.

It is to develop a reproducible mechanism through which permanent spaces can gradually be removed from speculation and returned to collective use.
